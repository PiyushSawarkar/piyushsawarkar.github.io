BhashaBench V1: A Comprehensive Benchmark for the Quadrant of Indic Domains

Domain-specific & multilingual benchmark — Agriculture, Legal, Finance and Ayurveda

Read abstract

The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has intensified the need for domain- and culture-specific evaluation. Existing benchmarks are largely Anglocentric and domain-agnostic, limiting their applicability to India-centric contexts. BhashaBench V1 addresses this gap as a large-scale, bilingual, exam-based benchmark covering four India-centric domains — Agriculture, Legal, Finance and Ayurveda. It contains 74,166 meticulously curated question-answer pairs (52,494 in English, 21,672 in Hindi) sourced from authentic government and domain-specific exams, spanning 90+ subdomains and 500+ topics for fine-grained evaluation. Evaluating 29+ LLMs reveals significant domain- and language-specific performance gaps, with especially large disparities in low-resource domains — for instance, GPT-4o reaches 76.49% accuracy in Legal but only 59.74% in Ayurveda, and models consistently perform better on English than Hindi across all domains. Subdomain analysis shows areas like Cyber Law and International Finance performing relatively well, while Panchakarma, Seed Science and Human Rights remain notably weak. BhashaBench V1 enables assessment of models' ability to integrate domain-specific knowledge with bilingual understanding.

The Art of Breaking Words: Rethinking Multilingual Tokenizer Design

Read abstract

While model architecture and training objectives are well-studied, tokenization — particularly in multilingual contexts — remains a relatively neglected aspect of LLM development. Existing tokenizers often exhibit high token-to-word ratios, inefficient use of context length, and slower inference. This work presents a systematic study linking vocabulary size, pre-tokenization rules and training-corpus composition to both token-to-word efficiency and model quality, grounded in extensive experiments on Indic scripts, which present unique challenges due to their script diversity and orthographic complexity. The proposed data-composition algorithm for balancing multilingual tokenizer training reduces the average token-to-word ratio by roughly 6% relative to conventional data randomization, and the resulting tokenizer achieves a 40%+ improvement in average token-to-word ratio against state-of-the-art multilingual Indic models — yielding measurable gains in both model performance and inference speed, and positioning tokenization as a critical lever for efficient, scalable multilingual LLMs alongside architecture and training objectives.

Param 1 Series — 2.9B Dense, 5B Dense & 7B MoE Text Foundation Models

Read abstract

Large Language Models have emerged as powerful general-purpose reasoning systems, yet their development remains dominated by English-centric data, architectures and optimization paradigms — a design that structurally under-represents linguistically diverse regions such as India, home to 20+ official languages, 100+ dialects, code-switching and diglossia. PARAM-1 is a 2.9B-parameter decoder-only, text-only language model trained from scratch with an explicit architectural and linguistic focus on Indian diversity, on a bilingual (Hindi–English) dataset built around fact-rich, high-quality content. It is guided by three core principles: equitable representation of Indic languages via a 25% corpus allocation; tokenization fairness through a SentencePiece tokenizer adapted to Indian morphological structures; and culturally aligned evaluation across IndicQA, code-mixed reasoning and socio-linguistic robustness tasks. By embedding diversity at the pretraining level rather than deferring it to post-hoc alignment, PARAM-1 offers a design-first blueprint for equitable foundation modeling, serving as both a competent general-purpose model and a robust baseline for India-centric applications.

Thesis Stage 2: Problems in Graph Sampling and Graph Learning

CS 798 Stage 2 · Academic Year 2023–24, Summer Semester · Dissertation, July 2024

Read abstract

We address the challenge of learning a graphical representation for the entities (nodes) present in a population, each characterized by its own multivariate features (signals). Traditionally in Graph Signal Processing (GSP), smoothness of the signals in the graph spectral domain was a ubiquitous assumption — true only if the observed signals resulted from a pure diffusion process. To be more pragmatic to real-world use cases, we propose a novel formulation for graph learning that instead encourages sparsity in the graph spectral domain, and rigorously test it on synthetic and real-world datasets. Additionally, since existing graph learning algorithms apply inconsistent thresholding of learned matrices as a post-processing step, we propose a principled, data-driven approach for hyperparameter selection applicable both to our formulation and to other GSP-based graph learning formulations in general.

Novel Algorithm — Graph Learning using Spectral Sparsity with Heterogeneous Support (HSGL)

Proposed as part of the Master's thesis

Read abstract

We present HSGL (Heterogeneous Support Graph Learning), an approach to infer the underlying edge structure of a graph given a set of nodes and a set of graph signals defined at every node. Most existing graph-learning techniques assume all signals are bandlimited in the underlying graph Fourier transform (GFT) basis. HSGL instead assumes the underlying signals merely have sparse support that may extend to high-frequency basis vectors, and that different signals may have different support sets. Under these conditions, we present a three-way alternating minimization strategy to simultaneously infer the graph signal coefficients, the GFT basis vectors, and the eigenvalues of the underlying Laplacian matrix, along with a purely data-driven strategy for hyperparameter selection. On synthetic data and a variety of real-world graphs, HSGL delivers superior learning results compared to the state of the art.

Thesis Stage 1: Graph Learning & Node Subset Selection Under Sparsity Constraint

CS 797 Stage 1 · Academic Year 2023–24 · Presentation, October 2023

Read abstract

This work explores the application of Graph Signal Processing (GSP) and compressed sensing to optimize epidemiological testing and contact tracing during viral outbreaks such as COVID-19 and measles. To address the NP-hard subset-selection problem of identifying the most informative nodes to test under limited resources, the study evaluates strategies including node degree, Kemeny-based centrality indicators and mutual coherence. Framing infection statuses as partially known graph signals, the framework uses convex optimization to reconstruct full population disease dynamics, demonstrating efficacy across real-world datasets such as the BMC Measles and American COVID-19 corpora. The thesis further extends into graph learning, proposing a novel alternating-optimization formulation to infer the underlying graph Laplacian directly from data signals when prior topological information is absent — balancing network sparsity with structural integrity via Stiefel manifolds and specialized L1 block-norm constraints to capture the true disease-transmission network without disconnected subgraphs.

Read abstract

This seminar work addresses the NP-hard subset-selection problem to optimize speedy mass testing and contact tracing during epidemics such as COVID-19 and measles. Industrial R&D — DH 307: Sparse Recovery for Population-Level Health Signals

Academic Year 2022–23, Spring Semester

Read abstract

This work presents a theoretical framework for efficient reconstruction and prediction of population-level health signals using ideas from compressed sensing, graph signal processing and image processing — recovering the health status of unobserved entities across a population from measurements over only a representative subset. Recognizing the problem as sparse signal recovery, the work develops LASSO-based compressed-sensing formulations together with principled subset-selection strategies based on graph structure, node degree, Kemeny indicators, mutual coherence and their combinations. The methodology was evaluated across real-world settings: with the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, applied to Johns Hopkins COVID-19 patient data, achieving under 15% RMSE using only 35% of the measurements; and with the Brihanmumbai Municipal Corporation (BMC) and MCGM, applied to mitigation and predictive modelling of Mumbai's measles outbreak to inform public-health decision-making. The framework was further extended with geographic mobility information to construct contact-tracing graphs and study disease propagation, while also revealing fundamental limitations of conventional GSP approaches in capturing individual-level disease transmission.

Modeling the Impact of Active HPV Testing Intervention on Cervical Cancer Burden in India

Dept. of CSE, IIT Bombay & National Disease Modelling Consortium (NDMC), Dept. of Mathematics, IIT Bombay · Study funded by the Gates Foundation

Read abstract

The global burden of mortality in women due to cervical cancer is relatively high in low- and middle-income countries (LMIC), particularly India, driven largely by persistent HPV infection. HPV testing plays a crucial role in identifying infections early, enabling timely intervention — yet no systematic modelling studies had investigated its role in reducing cervical cancer burden. This work develops a mathematical model to investigate the impact of active HPV testing, introducing an HPV-infected compartment to capture the dynamics of known vs. unknown infected individuals, tracking infection progression, recovery and cancer outcomes. Parameters are calibrated using literature and India-specific epidemiological data. Simulations across different testing ages and coverage rates show higher testing rates reduce cervical cancer prevalence and overall mortality, and an optimal-control approach using active testing coverage as a decision variable derives cost-effective strategies for minimizing treatment and testing expenses — underscoring the need for appropriate resource allocation and policy intervention.

Integrating Epidemiological Modeling with Optimal Control for Cost-Effective HPV Intervention Schedules

CS 692 · Academic Year 2023–24, Spring Semester · Collaboration: Institute for Disease Modeling (IDM), Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation; ICMR, Delhi

Read abstract

This work explores cost-effective intervention schedules for HPV disease mitigation, integrating epidemiological modeling and optimal control theory. Using the open-source agent-based model HPVsim, the research simulates disease progression and assesses public-health responses including prophylactic and therapeutic vaccination, cervical screening, and treatments such as thermal ablation and excisional procedures. Initial greedy analyses show female-only vaccination significantly reduces incidence, while gender-neutral vaccination should be applied sparingly; further non-greedy analyses show that integrating screening and treatment with vaccination balances immediate stability with future preparedness. Analysis of Cervical Cancer & HPV Transmission in India — Custom Screening, Treatment & Vaccination Strategy Design

CS 691 · Academic Year 2023–24, Autumn Semester · Collaboration: Institute for Disease Modeling (IDM), Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation; ICMR, Delhi

Read abstract

This work presents a comprehensive analysis of cervical cancer and HPV transmission dynamics in India using HPVsim, parameterized with India-specific demographic data, structured sexual mixing networks, co-transmitting HPV genotypes and high-resolution disease natural history, to evaluate the cost-effectiveness of public-health interventions. The study assesses standalone and combined strategies, including prophylactic vaccination across age brackets and gender splits, and the WHO's seven recommended screening-and-treatment (S&T) algorithms. CS 726 Advanced Machine Learning · Academic Year 2022–23, Spring Semester (Jan–May '23) · Guide: Prof. Sunita Sarawagi

Constraint based Generation with Diﬀusion Models using MCMC Sampling

Read abstract

While conditional generative models successfully ensure aligned outputs through condition-based loss training, the unconstrained nature of vanilla generative models often yields underwhelming results — yet enforcing specific generation constraints traditionally requires retraining from scratch, which is computationally prohibitive. This work introduces a training-free strategy that constrains the generation process of well-trained vanilla diffusion models using Langevin Monte Carlo (MCMC) sampling, embedding MCMC directly into the diffusion model's reverse process to iteratively course-correct the output toward a target distribution, driven by a continuous, user-defined scoring function with gradients backpropagated during denoising. We evaluate several integration techniques — Langevin Metropolis-Hastings at each timestep, denoised approximations for MCMC updates, and targeted guidance on predicted means via a feedback loop — and demonstrate, via tasks such as controlling color dominance in generated images, that MCMC sampling can repurpose vanilla diffusion models for highly constrained, condition-aligned generation without retraining.

CS 752 System Dynamics: Modeling & Simulation for Development · Academic Year 2022–23, Spring Semester · Guide: Prof. Om Damani

Simulating the Shadows: A System Dynamics Approach to Corruption and the Underground Economy

Read abstract

The persistent growth of the underground economy and endemic corruption significantly distorts national economic frameworks by eroding capital and encouraging illicit tax practices. This work presents a System Dynamics model simulating the dynamic interdependence between the subterranean and formal economies, integrating GDP dynamics, tax evasion and corruption losses, public infrastructure development, and workforce migration between honest and corrupt sectors. Simulation results show public infrastructure and GDP peak under high government efficacy and minimal corruption, though capable administrations can still foster legitimate growth despite marginal fund losses; excessive taxation deteriorates tax morale and fuels illicit labor, while moderate tax rates sustain long-term infrastructure growth. The simulations suggest a total crackdown on the shadow economy may be counterproductive, since illicit funds are often reinvested into the formal market — pointing policymakers toward gradual transitional strategies that integrate underground assets into the formal sector.

CS 631 Implementation techniques in DBMS · Academic Year 2022–23 · Guide: Prof. S. Sudarshan · Project 1

Catalyzing Data Integrity: Advancing Functional Dependency Support in PostgreSQL

Read abstract

Functional dependencies (FDs) are fundamental to database integrity but are rarely enforced by modern relational database management systems during data modification. This project introduces a validation mechanism embedded directly within the PostgreSQL backend to natively enforce single-attribute FD constraints, creating a dedicated relational catalog to store user-defined dependencies and dynamically intercepting INSERT and UPDATE queries to preserve data integrity — validating on insertion that a determinant isn't mapped to a new value if an association already exists, and systematically evaluating conditional cases on update to ensure state changes don't break existing constraints. Experimental results demonstrate successful prevention of anomalous data entries during both insertions and updates, establishing a scalable foundation extensible to multi-attribute FDs.

CartoonGAN: A GAN for Photo-to-Hayao-Miyazaki-Style Cartoon Translation

Read abstract

This project implemented and evaluated a Generative Adversarial Network designed to transfer the stylistic elements of Hayao Miyazaki's cartoons to real-world photographs. Building on the CartoonGAN methodology by Chen et al. (which uses unpaired datasets), this implementation used the Flickr30K dataset for real-world images and Safebooru for target cartoon styles, with a 14-layer generator (residual and up-convolution blocks) and a 5-layer PatchGAN discriminator. A combined loss integrated an adversarial loss — trained with edge-smoothed cartoon images to promote clear edges — and a content loss measured via VGG feature maps to preserve semantic structure, with multiple training iterations tuning the weighting parameter to balance aesthetic transformation against content preservation. The study proposed a comparative evaluation via user surveys against CartoonGAN and GANILLA for aesthetic appeal and "cartooniness."

Emotion-Aware Music Player Using Optimized Fisherface Feature Extraction & Cascaded AdaBoost Facial Recognition

Read abstract

Traditional recommendation platforms rely heavily on historical user tracking to influence media consumption, while standalone Viola-Jones face detection caps emotion-recognition accuracy around 70% due to lighting and pose sensitivity. This study presents an emotion-based music player powered by a combined Fisherface and Cascaded AdaBoost pipeline, extracting facial feature vectors via PCA for dimensionality reduction and LDA for optimal class separation. Facial frames are detected via Haar Cascade models, processed with CLAHE, and normalized to 350×350 pixels. To drastically reduce execution latency and memory overhead without sacrificing accuracy, the model transitions from computationally expensive macro-datasets to streamlined, user-specific training sets of 400–450 images from the Cohn-Kanade database. Real-time emotion prediction captures 10 sub-frames within 1–2 seconds to classify four core emotions — Happy, Sad, Angry and Neutral — and, via the Eel framework, the Python classification engine interfaces with an HTML/CSS/JavaScript web UI to trigger real-time, emotion-aligned music playback.

Autonomous Control in Flappy Bird via Lightweight Neuroevolution and Feedforward Perceptron Topologies

Read abstract

Real-time continuous obstacle avoidance in side-scrolling environments presents a fundamental control challenge for autonomous agents. This study demonstrates an automated decision-making system for Flappy Bird, powered by a custom neuroevolution framework combining Artificial Neural Networks with Genetic Algorithms. The core architecture is a compact feedforward MLP with a [2, 2, 1] topology; at each frame, agents process two normalized spatial features — current vertical coordinate and the height of the approaching pipe gap — via a logistic sigmoid activation, triggering a binary flap action above a 0.5 threshold. The genetic optimization loop manages 50 networks per generation, driven by 20% elitism retention, 20% random network injection, uniform single-point crossover, and 10% synaptic weight mutation within a ±0.5 interval. Rendered in an HTML5 canvas environment with fitness scored on frame-survival time, the system shows rapid generational convergence toward continuous obstacle navigation, demonstrating the efficiency of shallow neuroevolutionary models in real-time automated gameplay.
